## How to review

1. Open `batch-01.md` in a Markdown viewer and work through the batches in order.
2. For each task, read the A/B comparison table first and form your own view. Then read the AI-proposed label, note and evidence lists.
3. Tick one box after **Danny's decision** (☐ same ☐ different ☐ uncertain) and add comments in the `>` block. An unticked box means the task is not reviewed.
4. Where a field means something specific:
   - *Supporting evidence* is evidence **for the proposed label**; *Conflicting evidence* points the **other way**.
   - Italic `helper:` lines are exact-string comparisons computed by a script (normalized DOI equality, exact coauthor-name overlap, shared ROR). They are reading aids, not judgments. They miss accent and spelling variants (e.g. Vrgoc/Vrgoč, Jeff/Jeffrey), which the notes point out where relevant.
   - "OpenAlex-supplied" ORCIDs and OpenAlex author IDs are shown for transparency but were treated only as provider hypotheses.
5. When you have finished, tell me which decisions you made. I will not convert anything into final annotations or an official checkpoint until you explicitly ask.

## Policy applied

- **Evidence used:** only the `leftEvidence`/`rightEvidence` payloads in each task. `partition`, `stratum`, `groupIds` and `candidateRefs`/`blockKey` were not used. No matcher scores, predictions, model decisions or evaluation outputs were opened. No external lookups were made.
- **Work:**
  - Equal normalized DOI plus a compatible title → `same`. Online-vs-print year differences were accepted.
  - Distinct normalized DOIs → always flagged `IDENTIFIER_CONFLICT`. These were labeled `different` only when titles, authors and venues also differed clearly.
  - Version-related pairs → `uncertain` + `VERSION_RELATIONSHIP`, each judged on its own evidence. Example: a CONCUR DOI and an Information & Computation DOI carrying the same title (#150).
  - One side without a DOI → no conflict flag. The label came from title, author, year and venue evidence.
- **Researcher:**
  - Clearly different given names (e.g. Jing vs Jianguo, Ce vs Chao) were treated as affirmative evidence of `different`.
  - `same` required corroboration beyond the name, such as a coauthor in the payloads, a shared ROR, a specific topic match, or both mentions being the sole author of one DOI.
  - Name + shared ROR with **no** topic continuity or coauthor → `uncertain`.
  - Differing RORs or missing overlap were never used on their own to justify `different`.

## Limitations

- These are one model's judgments, and they reflect its reading of the agreed policy. Borderline Researcher cases depend on interpretation.
- ROR identifiers were compared by value only. Institution names were never inferred from ROR IDs, so a Crossref organization-name string and an OpenAlex ROR were treated as not comparable.
- Several titles are truncated in the source (e.g. "Technical Perspective:", "Cypher"), and the notes acknowledge it.
- Helper scripts, rendered evidence and the per-batch judgment files used to assemble these drafts live in a temporary session scratchpad outside this folder. `draft-labels.json` holds the complete content.
